### a Have a brief discussion in your group to decide on the scope of the domain of knowledge, that is, what sort of information and semantics would be inside or outside the domain.

Physical measurements of objects. So we need to define the object being measured, the quantity of that property, the unit of the property, the uncertanty in that measure, and which property was measured

### b Think of some knowledge-based system(s), database(s), or other digital platform(s) employing semantic technology that could be developed within the domain of knowledge as you understand it. Now imagine that your work on the domain ontology is part of the overall development work on this system or these systems.

Could have a database of commonly used physical quantitives

### c Draft three personas, that is, abstractions of representative groups of stakeholders (users, developers, or similar) interacting with the system. The idea of a persona here is the one from agile requirements analysis.

Chemist, engineer, student



### d Three members of your group will each impersonate one persona and formulate two competency questions each, so your group has six competency questions. This is much fewer than usual in such processes; it will allow you to fulfill all requirements and still have a small ontology.

#### Chemist
- Which measurements have less than 10% uncertanty?
- What is the given measurments uncertanty?
#### Engineer
- What is the smallest possible value an object can have, given its uncertanty?
- What is the smallest value of uncertanty an object can have given certain circumstances?

#### Student
- what are the values to the properties to the objects?
- which unit is the measure of the property given for a given object?

### e Polish the CQs a bit and agree on them as a group. After this process, effectively, your definition of the domain of knowledge and scope of your ontology is "that which is needed to express the six competency questions."
